## Purpose — required, verbatim
> "Reach for this when you have a specific ask (a yes, a signature, a referral, a hire, a purchase) and the other side is undecided or leaning away. Decision cue: if you can name the exact behavior you want and the person who must do it, run this. If you are still discovering what you want, stop and define the ask first." — .claude/skills/business-strategy/references/influence.md:5

## Design intent — required
Distills Robert Cialdini's persuasion methodology ("Influence") into an ethical, decision-tree-guided protocol for framing specific business asks (sales closes, hiring decisions, partnership agreements, reviews). It matches relationship states to one of seven core principles (Reciprocity, Commitment, Social Proof, Liking, Authority, Scarcity, Unity), emphasizing that triggers must be earned honestly and not manufactured. It enforces single-principle focus or complementary pairing, concrete low-friction next actions, honest stating of the cost of inaction, and explicitly leaving the exit open ("you're free to decide"). Without this reference, agents and communicators tend to muddy messages by haphazardly stacking manipulative persuasion heuristics that erode trust, trigger reactance, or breach ethical standards.

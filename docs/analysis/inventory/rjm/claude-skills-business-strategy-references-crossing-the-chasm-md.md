# .claude/skills/business-strategy/references/crossing-the-chasm.md

## Purpose — required, verbatim
> "A go-to-market method for technology products that sold to visionaries but stalled before the mainstream. The fix is focus: win one narrow segment so completely that its references pull the next segment in." — .claude/skills/business-strategy/references/crossing-the-chasm.md:3-5

## Design intent — required
Distills Geoffrey Moore's "Crossing the Chasm" technology adoption lifecycle methodology for tech companies that achieved early traction with visionary adopters but stalled before breaking into the conservative pragmatist mainstream. It guides teams to resist the temptation to chase disparate customer segments simultaneously, mandating a concentrated beachhead strategy: dominating a single narrow niche, constructing the complete "whole product" (integrations, support, partnerships), crafting a compelling reason to buy against existing alternatives, securing peer reference customers, and expanding outward using a "bowling pin" strategy. Without this reference, startups spreading resources across multiple vertical markets fail to attain the reference density required by risk-averse mainstream enterprise buyers.

## Inputs — required

- Beachhead market candidates (problem urgency, size, reachability via known channels, reference potential).
- Incumbent alternative solutions used by the segment.

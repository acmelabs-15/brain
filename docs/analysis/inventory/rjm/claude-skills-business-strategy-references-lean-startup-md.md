## Purpose — required, verbatim
> "Distilled from Eric Ries, The Lean Startup. The method treats a new venture as a series of experiments, not a plan to execute. You are not testing whether you can build the thing. You are testing whether anyone wants it." — .claude/skills/business-strategy/references/lean-startup.md:3-5

## Design intent — required
Distills Eric Ries's "The Lean Startup" methodology for entrepreneurs and product teams developing offerings under conditions of extreme uncertainty. It reframes venture building from executing a fixed plan to rapidly cycling through the Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop. It emphasizes identifying the leap-of-faith assumption, designing falsifiable experiments before coding, deploying genuine minimum viable products (including concierge or landing page MVPs) that test customer willingness to pay or engage, measuring through cohort-based actionable metrics rather than vanity metrics, and making deliberate pivot-or-persevere decisions. Without this reference, teams waste months or years engineering polished technical solutions for products that fail in the market due to a complete absence of customer demand.
